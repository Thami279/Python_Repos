## AJNR: American Journal of Neuroradiology

- **[Patient-Reported Barriers to Care in Spinal Cerebrospinal Fluid Leak: A Cross-Sectional Survey](https://doi.org/10.3174/ajnr.A9459)** — *Original Research* | June 8, 2026
  Spinal CSF leak remains a condition plagued by prolonged diagnostic delays and fragmented care, and this survey is the first to systematically characterize the patient-reported barriers driving those delays. Key findings include poor clinician awareness, lack of specialist access, and inadequate imaging protocols as the dominant obstacles. For neuroradiologists, this underscores the need to maintain a low threshold for dynamic myelography and CT myelography when spinal CSF leak is clinically suspected.

- **[A Potential Adjunctive Role of 7T MRI and Amino-Acid PET in Localizing MRI-Occult Functional Pituitary Adenomas](https://doi.org/10.3174/ajnr.A9448)** — *Review* | June 8, 2026
  Functional pituitary adenomas (particularly Cushing's microadenomas) that are negative on standard 1.5T/3T MRI pose a major diagnostic challenge, and this review examines whether 7T MRI combined with amino-acid PET can rescue occult lesions before invasive inferior petrosal sinus sampling. The pooled evidence suggests meaningful incremental sensitivity, particularly for lesions <6 mm. This is directly relevant to any centre involved in pituitary MRI reporting, guiding when to escalate the workup.

- **[The 2026 AHA/ASA Guideline Updates to Management of Patients with Acute Ischemic Stroke: A Guide for Neuroradiologists](https://doi.org/10.3174/ajnr.A9455)** — *Review* | June 4, 2026
  The 2026 guideline revision updates imaging thresholds for late-window thrombectomy, expands indications for tenecteplase, and introduces refined recommendations on CT perfusion interpretation and patient selection. This neuroradiologist-focused guide translates the clinical guideline changes into actionable imaging criteria for everyday stroke calls. Essential reading for any radiologist who reads acute stroke imaging or participates in stroke codes.

- **[Long-Term Vascular Remodeling After Headache-Onset Intracranial Vertebral Artery Dissection](https://doi.org/10.3174/ajnr.A9446)** — *Original Research* | June 4, 2026
  Vertebral artery dissection presenting with isolated headache (without ischemia) has an undercharacterized long-term course; this study maps the spectrum of chronic MRA outcomes including recanalization, pseudoaneurysm formation, and residual stenosis over years of follow-up. The data provide the first robust natural history framework to guide follow-up imaging intervals. Neuroradiologists interpreting serial vascular MRI in these patients will find this directly applicable.

- **[Factors Associated with Poor Outcome after Successful Reperfusion in Patients with Large Infarct: A Multicenter Study](https://doi.org/10.3174/ajnr.A9456)** — *Original Research* | June 3, 2026
  Despite successful thrombectomy, many large-core stroke patients have poor outcomes, and this multicenter study identifies the imaging predictors — including final infarct volume, hemorrhagic transformation grade, and edema index — that best stratify prognosis post-EVT. The findings refine how radiologists should frame prognostic language in post-thrombectomy reports. Direct relevance for anyone writing follow-up imaging reports after mechanical thrombectomy.

- **[A Comprehensive 2.5-Minute Brain MRI Protocol in the Pediatric Emergency Department: Diagnostic Evaluation Without Sedation](https://doi.org/10.3174/ajnr.A9454)** — *Original Research* | June 3, 2026
  This study validates an ultrafast 2.5-minute brain MRI protocol — leveraging deep learning reconstruction and simultaneous multi-slice acquisition — achieving diagnostic equivalence to standard protocols in pediatric ED patients without sedation. The protocol detected all significant intracranial pathology while eliminating the need for GA or prolonged scanner time. A potentially practice-changing result for paediatric neuroimaging services.

- **[CT-to-FLAIR Style Transfer Improves Perilesional Edema Conspicuity in Brain Metastases](https://doi.org/10.3174/ajnr.A9457)** — *Original Research* | June 3, 2026
  A GAN-based style transfer model trained to synthesize FLAIR-like images from non-contrast CT significantly improved detection and characterisation of perilesional edema in brain metastases compared to CT alone. The synthetic FLAIR achieved AUC comparable to real MRI for edema grading in this 321-patient cohort. This has immediate implications for centres managing patients who cannot undergo MRI but require metastasis surveillance.

- **[Noninvasive Volume Measurement of Cerebral AVM Nidus: Silent MRA vs TOF-MRA and DSA](https://doi.org/10.3174/ajnr.A9370)** — *Original Research* | June 3, 2026
  Accurate AVM nidus volumetry is critical for Spetzler-Martin grading and radiosurgery planning; this study shows Silent MRA (non-contrast, flow-independent) performs comparably to TOF-MRA and closely approximates DSA-derived volumes. The technique removes flow-related artifacts that can mis-size the nidus on TOF. Valuable for neuroradiologists reporting AVM cases where optimal planning data is needed non-invasively.

- **[Treatment of Middle Cerebral Artery Aneurysms with Flow-Diverter Stents: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.3174/ajnr.A9188)** — *Systematic Review* | June 3, 2026
  This meta-analysis compiles safety and efficacy data on flow diversion for MCA aneurysms — a historically controversial indication due to branch incorporation risk. Complete occlusion rates were favourable (~75%) with acceptable morbidity when patient selection accounted for side-branch anatomy. Provides evidence to support nuanced case-by-case recommendations in multidisciplinary aneurysm conferences.

- **[Indeterminate T1-Hypointense Spine Lesions: Prevalence of Malignancy in the Absence of Aggressive Imaging Features](https://doi.org/10.3174/ajnr.A9180)** — *Original Research* | June 3, 2026
  T1-hypointense vertebral lesions without aggressive features are ubiquitous on spine MRI yet lack management consensus; this large retrospective study quantifies the risk of occult malignancy and identifies imaging features that reliably predict benignity. The data support a watch-and-wait strategy for a defined subset, reducing unnecessary follow-up imaging. Directly applicable to the daily challenge of characterising incidental bone lesions on spine studies.

- **[DANTE-SPACE vs Conventional SPACE for Post-Contrast Intracranial Vessel Wall MRI](https://doi.org/10.3174/ajnr.A9452)** — *Original Research* | June 3, 2026
  DANTE blood suppression prepulse significantly improves intraluminal signal suppression over conventional SPACE for vessel wall MRI, enabling better wall enhancement characterisation in intracranial vasculopathy. Signal-to-noise and contrast-to-noise ratios were superior without a meaningful acquisition time penalty. Relevant for neuroradiologists building or optimizing vessel wall MRI protocols.

- **[Braided Versus Laser-Cut Stents in Venous Sinus Stenting for Pulsatile Tinnitus: A Systematic Review](https://doi.org/10.3174/ajnr.A9451)** — *Systematic Review* | June 3, 2026
  Venous sinus stenting for pulsatile tinnitus from sigmoid/transverse sinus stenosis is growing in adoption; this review compares procedural success, symptom resolution, and complication profiles between braided and laser-cut stent designs. Both achieve high symptom relief rates, but braided stents show more complete sinus remodelling at follow-up imaging. Provides the best available evidence base for centre-level stent selection decisions.

- **[Perfusion Parameters to Identify Carotid Artery Disease Aetiology in Acute Ischaemic Stroke Before EVT](https://doi.org/10.3174/ajnr.A9156)** — *Original Research* | June 3, 2026
  CT perfusion maps acquired pre-thrombectomy carry information about the underlying ICA lesion type (atherosclerosis vs dissection vs cardioembolism); this study defines perfusion thresholds that non-invasively predict aetiology before catheter angiography. A positive finding for integrating perfusion imaging into the mechanistic workup rather than purely patient selection. Impacts how we report and contextualise CTP findings in the acute stroke report.

- **[Neuronal Surface and Intracellular Antibodies in Mild Cognitive Impairment Due to Alzheimer Disease: Prevalence and Phenotype](https://doi.org/10.3174/ajnr.A9159)** — *Original Research* | June 3, 2026
  This study investigates whether autoantibodies typically seen in autoimmune encephalitis are also present in pathologically confirmed Alzheimer's MCI, and whether their presence modifies the imaging phenotype. A subset of MCI patients harboured detectable autoantibodies with atypical atrophy patterns, raising the possibility of a treatable overlay. Relevant for neuroradiologists encountering atypical dementia imaging who may flag an autoimmune differential.

- **[CMV-Related vs Genetic Hearing Loss in Children: Cortical Volumetric MRI Analysis](https://doi.org/10.3174/ajnr.A9176)** — *Original Research* | June 3, 2026
  Comparing children with congenital CMV-related versus genetic sensorineural hearing loss, this study demonstrates distinct patterns of cortical volume loss and hemispheric asymmetry at brain MRI, reflecting differential impacts of peripheral auditory deprivation versus central CMV encephalopathy. The findings support including brain MRI volumetrics in the workup of paediatric hearing loss, particularly when CMV aetiology is suspected.

## AJNR: Clinical Practice

- **[Gadolinium-Based Contrast Agents in the Follow-up of Pituitary Adenomas Following Transsphenoidal Surgery](https://doi.org/10.3174/ajnr.A9458)** — *Clinical Practice* | June 4, 2026
  This evidence review challenges the reflex use of gadolinium for every post-operative pituitary MRI, showing that in patients with no clinical suspicion of recurrence beyond the immediate post-op phase, unenhanced sequences provide equivalent diagnostic information for most adenoma subtypes. The authors propose a contrast-rationalisation algorithm based on clinical context and adenoma type. Practical guidance for protocol optimisation and reducing unnecessary gadolinium exposure.

- **[Mapping the Facial Nerve Beyond the Skull Base: DESS vs SPACE MRI Sequences](https://doi.org/10.3174/ajnr.A9439)** — *Clinical Practice* | June 4, 2026
  Accurate extracranial facial nerve mapping is essential for parotid surgery planning; this head-to-head comparison of DESS and SPACE sequences shows DESS provides superior nerve-to-background contrast in the extracranial segments, enabling more reliable perineural tracking. The study directly informs sequence selection for parotid and skull base pre-surgical MRI protocols. Useful for neuroradiologists involved in head and neck imaging.

- **[ACR-ASNR-ASSR-SIR-SNIS Practice Parameter for Image-Guided Epidural Steroid Injections](https://doi.org/10.3174/ajnr.A9280)** — *Practice Parameter* | June 3, 2026
  A multi-society updated practice parameter covering patient selection, informed consent, technique, and post-procedure management for fluoroscopic and CT-guided epidural steroid injections. The update refines anticoagulation management guidelines and addresses newer injectate formulations. Mandatory reference for any neuroradiology spine intervention programme.

- **[Clinical Evaluation of Deep Learning-Reconstructed Postcontrast 3D T1-Weighted VIBE for Internal Auditory Canal Pathology](https://doi.org/10.3174/ajnr.A9189)** — *Comparative Study* | June 3, 2026
  Deep learning reconstruction allows postcontrast 3D T1 VIBE acquisitions to be shortened without compromising diagnostic quality for IAC pathology (acoustic neuroma, facial nerve enhancement); reader performance was non-inferior to standard-duration sequences for lesion detection and characterisation. This supports adopting DL-reconstructed fast VIBE in routine clinical IAC protocols. Immediately applicable for practices with DL reconstruction tools.
